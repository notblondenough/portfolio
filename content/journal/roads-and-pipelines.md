*This is a sample entry, published so the journal has a shape before it has a history. Real posts will replace it.*

Most of my work happens where nobody's looking. A Kafka topic fills and drains at three in the morning. An agent reads a screen no human will ever see, writes down what it found, and hands the note to the next agent in line. When it all works, the only evidence is an absence — a week of manual effort that simply doesn't happen anymore.

That's the kind of engineering I like: long roads, built carefully, that other things travel on.

This journal is for the parts of that work worth writing down. Expect posts about distributed systems and the odd ways they fail, about evaluating AI systems honestly, about what I'm learning as the tooling around LLMs hardens into real infrastructure. Occasionally something about competitive programming, which is where I first learned that a problem read slowly is a problem half-solved.

The site itself borrows its light from *Paris, Texas* — bleached daylight up top, motel neon by the time you reach the contact form. Partly because I love the film. Mostly because its patience feels right for the work: long silences, big skies, and the sense that the distance itself is the point.

More soon. The desk light's on.
